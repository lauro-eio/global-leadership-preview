Fair by What Standard: What the Evidence Says About Objective Criteria in Everyday Negotiation

Fair by What Standard: What the Evidence Says About Objective Criteria in Everyday Negotiation

Splitting the difference feels fair and often isn't. Negotiation analysis shows settlements anchored to external, verifiable standards tend to be more durable and less resentful (Raiffa, 1982).

Why "feel fair" fails

Without a shared yardstick, each side uses different fairness scripts — equality, equity, need — and talks past each other (Deutsch, 1975). On a dock, "meet in the middle" on a detention fee can violate a contract clock both sides already signed.

External standards

Useful floor standards: posted appointment windows, rate cards, SOPs, safety lockout rules, SLA response times, published lead times. Raiffa's work emphasizes that parties who can point to a checkable reference settle with less residual conflict (Raiffa, 1982).

Process legitimacy

People accept outcomes more readily when the process cites a legitimate standard (Lind & Tyler, 1988). Naming the standard early is a process move, not a delay tactic.

Floor practice

Before bargaining a number, name the document or rule you will use. If none exists, agree how you will choose a standard — then negotiate.

Selected references

  • Deutsch, M. (1975). Equity, equality, and need. Journal of Social Issues, 31(3), 137–149.
  • Lind, E. A., & Tyler, T. R. (1988). The Social Psychology of Procedural Justice. Plenum.
  • Raiffa, H. (1982). The Art and Science of Negotiation. Harvard University Press.

The Split-the-Difference Detention Call I Should Have Documented

The Split-the-Difference Detention Call I Should Have Documented

I split a detention fee "to keep the relationship." The carrier left happy. Finance later showed the appointment log cleared us. I taught the carrier that tone beats paperwork.

Now I put the standard on the table first: "Let's open the appointment log and the contract window together." Sometimes we still concede — but as a choice, not because I was afraid of conflict.

Take-away · Key notes

Key Notes — Anchor to a Standard You Can Both Check

Key Notes — Anchor to a Standard You Can Both Check

Unit goal: Anchor floor negotiations to objective criteria instead of volume or 50/50 splits.

Core ideas

  • Feel-fair scripts conflict (Deutsch, 1975).
  • External standards settle cleaner (Raiffa, 1982).
  • Name the standard early for process legitimacy (Lind & Tyler, 1988).

Checklist

  1. Identify the governing document or rule.
  2. Open it together.
  3. Negotiate only gaps the standard does not cover.
  4. If conceding, label it as a concession off the standard — not a new truth.
